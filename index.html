CLP Holdings — FY2020–FY2025 Financial Trend & Ratio Analysis

I picked CLP because a capital-intensive utility actually rewards this kind of exercise. Its FY2020 to FY2025 numbers include a real earnings shock, a dividend that never got cut, and two different profit measures that tell almost opposite stories in the same year. This dashboard is the "what"; the written analysis is the "so what."

Data as of: FY2025 results, reported 26 Feb 2026. Figures in HK$ unless noted.

Six-year trends

FY2020–FY2025, on the most comparable disclosed basis for each metric.

Revenue

HK$bn · 2020–2025

2022's revenue spike looks good until you know where it came from: high Australian wholesale power prices that CLP was forced to buy at, not sell at profitably. The earnings chart next to this one shows why revenue and profit went in opposite directions that year.

Total Earnings vs. Operating Earnings

HK$bn · solid = statutory profit, dashed = underlying/core business

Total Earnings (statutory) Operating Earnings (underlying)

2022's near-wipeout in Total Earnings came from a hedging and outage shock in EnergyAustralia, not a collapse of the core business. Toggle to "Operating Earnings" above to see the much steadier story underneath it.

Dividend per share

HK$ · 2020–2025

Went up every single year shown here, even in 2022 when statutory profit fell more than 90%. Pretty typical regulated-utility behavior: smooth the one-off shocks so shareholders barely notice them.

Return on equity (ROE)

% · circle = calculated here, diamond = third-party estimate

Calculated as Total Earnings divided by year-end equity attributable to shareholders. The 2024 point (diamond marker) is a third-party estimate on a slightly different basis, so read it as directional rather than a clean match to the other years.
